# Spike blog writing guidelines

Apply these rules every time you write or rewrite blog copy for Spike. Check the draft against the checklist at the end before you show it.

These rules are a direction, not a law. You can break one when it makes sense, for example when a rule would make a sentence awkward or hurt clarity. When you break a rule on purpose, say which one and why when you show the draft.

## Voice

- Write plain-language points that feel human, not templated.
- Use a UK-style softer tone with hedging language. Suggest, don't instruct.
- Favour everyday verbs ("use", "test", "measure") over abstract nouns ("utilization", "implementation").
- Keep on-call sounding manageable, not scary (see word choices below).

## Sentence rules

- Use as few dependent clauses as you can. Break a long thought into two sentences.
- Use no more than one comma in a sentence. The exception is a list of items inside a sentence.
- Vary sentence length. Mix short (not very short) statements with longer ones. Avoid strings of short, choppy sentences that read like bullet points. Keep every sentence neither too short nor too long.
- Avoid excessive present-continuous tense (-ing words).
- Avoid em dashes completely.
- Avoid instructional language.
  - Bad: "Start with a 2-day rotation."
  - Good: "A 2-day rotation is a good place to start."

## Formatting

- Skip bracketed citations and rigid numbering.
- When you list items, use simple dashes or bullets.

## Banned words and phrases

- Never use: ensure, enhance, leverage, utilize, or any variation of them (ensures, ensuring, enhanced, leveraging, utilizes and so on).
- Never use: "tends".
- Never use: "for example" and "imagine".
- Never use: "most teams", "many teams", "some teams". This also means "most teams prefer" and "many teams prefer" are out, even though they sound like hedges.
- Use "incident triggers" instead of "incident happens" or "incident occurs".

## Word choices around on-call

Do not use negative, scary words such as "draining" or "relentless". They make on-call sound frightening, and we don't want that. Use subtler words such as "overwhelming" instead.

## Hedging language

Use phrases like these to soften instructions and claims:

- probably
- often helps
- consider
- you'll find suggestions on
- it can be helpful
- good luck
- go ahead and try it out
- usually works best
- it can be useful to

Vary them. Do not repeat the same hedging expression more than once in one article.

## Spike house rules (from the SEO workspace)

- Use "alert" and "alerting", not "page" or "paging".
- Write "Spike", not "Spike.sh", in prose.
- The content writer is Khushi (spell it exactly).

## Checklist before you show a draft

1. No em dashes anywhere.
2. No sentence has more than one comma (unless it is a list).
3. No banned words or phrases (ensure, enhance, leverage, utilize and variations; tends; for example; imagine; most/many/some teams).
4. No imperatives that read as instructions. Rephrase as a suggestion.
5. Few -ing words and few dependent clauses.
6. Sentence lengths vary, with no run of very short sentences.
7. Each hedging phrase appears at most once in the piece.
8. Lists use plain dashes or bullets. No bracketed citations.
9. No scary on-call words.
