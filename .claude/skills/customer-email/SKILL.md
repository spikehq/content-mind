# Writing Customer Emails & Follow-ups

Source: guidance from Kaushik (founder), shared in Slack 2026-08-30. Apply this whenever drafting a customer email, customer-story outreach, or a follow-up/reply in an existing thread.

## Core principle: Email is Business

Email is not a transactional notice, it's a conversation tending toward business. Don't write like you're filing a form. Write like you're talking to a person, but every sentence should still be moving toward a business outcome.

Reference for tone: x.com/TechEmails (leaked internal tech-industry emails, e.g. Steve Jobs). The common thread is that they're crisp — short sentences, no padding, no throat-clearing.

## The 5 parts of an email

1. Greeting
2. Intro
3. Main body
4. Thanks (sign-off line — often skipped, see below)
5. Signature

## 1. Greeting

- American recipient: `Hey <Name>`
- Everyone else: `Hi <Name>`
- If you genuinely don't know who you're addressing or their background: `Hello` — but this is rare, avoid defaulting to it.
- No comma after the greeting word: `Hi James,` not `Hi, James.`
- When replying inside an existing thread, skip the greeting entirely and jump straight into the message. Don't re-greet on every reply.

## 2. Intro

Don't just declare your title and hope the reader infers why that matters (e.g. "I am content marketer at Spike" is flat). Add friendliness/context to the role instead: "I look after all things content @ Spike."

Americans: skip "how are you" — go straight from greeting into the intro and the reason for writing.

```
Hey Sreekar

I am Kaushik, founder of Spike. I noticed you have been using Spike for .....
```

Non-Americans (including Canadians): a warmth line after the intro is expected — either works:

```
Hi Sreekar,

I am Kaushik, founder of Spike. It's nice to meet you. I noticed you have been...
```
or
```
Hi Sreekar,

I am Kaushik, founder of Spike. How are you? I noticed you have been...
```

Note: "how are you" in this context is a politeness convention (the English way of asking without really caring), not a request for a real answer — don't treat it as a question that needs answering before you get to business.

## 3. Main body

Subjective, depends on the ask. But keep it tight:

- 2 paragraphs is a great email, including all the details. If you're at 4+ paragraphs, you're probably over-explaining.
- Paragraphs (not one big block) are good.
- Keep sentences short. Break long comma-chained sentences into two shorter ones.
- Mention only 1-2 specific details when referencing something about the customer/their account. Listing everything you know about their usage reads as surveillance, not personalization.
- Don't repeat the same point twice in different words (e.g. "you're welcome to suggest changes" + "happy to make them" — pick one).
- When offering to make "changes," anchor what the changes are to ("changes to the story," not just "changes") — unanchored, it can misread as an offer to change the product itself.
- Don't restate something already implied earlier in the same email.
- Tone sits between casual and formal — not stiff, not chatty.
- No em dashes, anywhere, including inside lists. Use commas or split into separate sentences.
- Avoid merit/reward framing ("X deserves a spotlight") — being featured isn't a lottery. Prefer plain, direct phrasing ("We'd love to feature X on our customer wall").
- For invitations (e.g. customer story / wall of fame asks): frame it as inviting them into something that already exists, not building something new ("we have a wall of customer stories and we'd love to invite you to be part of it," not "we're building a wall"). Lead with genuine gratitude. Avoid over-the-top closers ("we'd be honored") — plainer is better ("We'd be glad to feature your story").

## 4. Thanks / sign-off line

This is the line right before the signature, and it's the piece most people get wrong.

- `Thanks` — the safe, universal default. Works everywhere.
- `Cheers` — only in follow-ups, or once the business/ask is concluded. Don't open with it.
- **No sign-off at all** — use this whenever you're expecting a reply, which is most emails. Instead of "Thanks," end with a pointer that hands the conversation back to the other person, so the reply is initiated by them:
  - "What do you think?"
  - "Let me know how you'd like to proceed."
  - "Happy to take questions."

  A closing "Thanks" reads as if the conversation is done. If you want a reply, don't close the door.

## 5. Signature

Whatever the standard signature is. Nothing special here beyond keeping it consistent.

## After the first email: treat it like SMS, not email

Once a thread is underway, stop treating it like formal email:

- Skip re-greeting.
- Skip repeating "Thanks" on every message.
- Reply fast. Speed is the actual lever here: "the faster you respond, the more business you get done." Aim for replies within 5 minutes, 15 minutes max.

## Worked example (customer story outreach)

This is the template Kaushik rewrote live as the reference case:

```
Hi Shaun,

Thank you for agreeing to this. We're looking for more of a short customer story than a one-liner.
If you could share your experience with Spike, especially around on-call and Alert Routing rules and how they've helped your team, that'd be great.

Once you share your thoughts, I'll shape them into a customer story and send the link to you. You can review it and let me know if you need any changes. Here's an example of what we did for one of our customers: https://spike.sh/customers/neat

What do you think?
```

Notice: greeting with no comma-before-name issue, 2 tight paragraphs, one concrete example link, no "Thanks" sign-off since a reply is expected, closes with a question that puts the ball in their court.

## Example link to use

When linking a sample customer story, use **https://spike.sh/customers/neat**. Kaushik called this out as the best customer story Spike has and asked for it to be the one shared with customers going forward, not the older RevX one (spike.sh/customers/revx). Always use Neat unless Kaushik names a new favorite.

## Quick checklist before sending

- [ ] Greeting matches the recipient's region (Hey / Hi), no comma after the name
- [ ] Intro states role with a bit of warmth, not just a dry title
- [ ] 2 paragraphs, short sentences, no em dashes
- [ ] Only 1-2 specific details if referencing their account/usage
- [ ] No redundant restatement of the same point
- [ ] If expecting a reply: ends with a question/pointer, not "Thanks"
- [ ] If this is a reply in an existing thread: no re-greeting, no repeated "Thanks," sent fast
- [ ] If linking a sample customer story, it's spike.sh/customers/neat, not revx
